Gold, plainly explained

The questions people are shy to ask.

Buying gold should not rest on trusting someone’s word. Here is how purity, weight and charges actually work, so you can check any jeweller, including us.

Standard levels

What the numbers mean.

Carat is how much of the metal is actually gold. The three digit number stamped beside it says the same thing in parts per thousand.

One tola is 11.664 grams. Rates are usually quoted both per tola and per ten grams, and the two should always agree with each other. If they do not, ask why.

Copper is what makes gold look warmer. More copper pulls a piece towards rose, more silver pulls it paler. Two pieces at the same carat can look different colours, and both can be honest.

Carat means two different things. For gold it is purity, out of 24. For a diamond or a coloured stone it is weight. Same word, two meanings, and they get mixed up constantly.

Questions

Ask us any of this at the counter.

How do I know the gold is really 22 carat?

By the stamp, not by our word. Purity is punched into the metal, usually on the clasp, the inside of a bangle, or the back of a pendant. Ask us to point it out on the piece in your hand. If a piece carries no mark, ask why before you buy it, here or anywhere.

What are making charges and wastage, really?

Making is the labour of turning raw gold into a finished piece. Wastage covers the small amount of gold genuinely lost in cutting, filing and polishing. Both are normal and every jeweller charges them. What is not normal is hiding them inside one number.

We quote them separately before you decide, and they appear as separate lines on the bill. Detailed work costs more to make, a plain chain costs less. Neither charge comes back when you sell, so it is worth knowing what you are paying at the start.

What will I get back if I sell it later?

The gold value at that day’s rate, for the weight and purity the piece actually is. Making and wastage do not come back, because that money went into labour.

Bring gold in and it is weighed and tested in front of you first. You see the weight and the purity reading yourself, and the number is worked out from there.

Can I exchange old jewellery for new?

Yes, and most people do. Old gold is tested and weighed in front of you, valued at the day’s rate, and set against the new piece. You pay the difference plus the making. The old bill helps but is not required.

How is the price actually calculated?

Four things, in this order. The day’s rate for that purity, multiplied by the weight. Then the making charge. Then wastage. Then any stone cost, which is priced separately from the gold because stones are not sold by gold weight.

Ask for each number on its own. Any jeweller who will not break it down is telling you something.

Do you make pieces to order?

Yes. Send a photograph on WhatsApp and we will quote the weight, the making and the time. Most pieces take one to three weeks, and bridal sets in wedding season need longer. Full steps are on the custom orders page.

Do you do repairs and polishing?

Yes. Re-stringing, clasp repairs, re-sizing, polishing and rhodium work. Bring the piece in and we will tell you what it needs and what it costs before we start.

Is there pressure to buy once I walk in?

No. Sit down, look at as many trays as you like, and leave without buying. People often come three or four times before a wedding purchase, and that is normal.
